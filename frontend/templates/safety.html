Stay prepared. Stay informed. Stay safe.

1. What to Do During a Flood

  • Move immediately to higher ground or a safe elevated location.
  • Switch off electricity and gas only if it is safe to do so.
  • Carry your emergency kit and important documents.
  • Call 112 and clearly communicate your exact location.
  • Follow official evacuation instructions.
  • Help children, elderly people and persons with disabilities if it is safe.

2. Never Enter Floodwater

  • Never walk, swim or drive through floodwater.
  • Floodwater may contain hidden drains, debris and dangerous currents.
  • Stay away from electric poles and fallen power lines.
  • Never touch electrical equipment while standing in water.
  • Keep away from open drains, bridges and damaged roads.

4. Safety Advice Based on Flood Risk

Follow the guidance corresponding to your district's predicted risk level.

LOW RISK

  • Stay informed about weather updates.
  • Keep emergency contacts saved.
  • Prepare your emergency kit.

MODERATE RISK

  • Prepare to move to a safer location.
  • Keep important documents protected.
  • Monitor official flood warnings.
  • Be ready to evacuate.

HIGH RISK

  • Follow evacuation orders immediately.
  • Move to higher ground.
  • Stay away from flooded areas.
  • Call 112 if you need emergency assistance.

Risk classifications are decision-support information. Always follow official government warnings and evacuation orders.

5. Safety Measures After a Flood

  • Return home only when authorities confirm it is safe.
  • Drink only boiled or properly purified water.
  • Discard food that has come into contact with floodwater.
  • Get electrical wiring and appliances inspected before switching power on.
  • Avoid damaged buildings, roads and bridges.
  • Wear protective footwear and gloves while cleaning.
  • Seek medical assistance if you develop symptoms after exposure to floodwater.

Your Safety Comes First

Stay alert, follow official instructions and never take unnecessary risks during a flood.
